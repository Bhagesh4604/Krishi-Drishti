More than 86\% of India's 600 million farming households cultivate plots under two hectares---plots too small to justify the USD \$3,000 per-farm cost of conventional carbon credit verification. This economic wall has kept one of the world's largest farming communities entirely shut out of a global voluntary carbon market worth USD \$2 billion. We built \textbf{Krishi-Drishti} (literally, ``Farmer's Vision'') to tear down that wall. At its heart is a satellite-driven Digital Measurement, Reporting, and Verification (dMRV) engine: Sentinel-2 Level-2A multispectral imagery is pulled from Google Earth Engine, cloud-masked, and fed into a three-model Stacking Ensemble (Random Forest, XGBoost, CatBoost) that predicts Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) with R$^2$\,=\,0.89 and RMSE\,=\,1.52\,g/kg across 50 field-sampled Indian plots---no laboratory required. Two companion modules round out the platform: a MobileNetV2 crop disease classifier (88.1\% accuracy) backed by a Gemini 2.5 Flash language model for organic-first treatment advice, and a peer-to-peer Mandi produce marketplace. Pilot testing with five farmers returned an 84.0/100 System Usability Scale score, and our economic model projects a supplementary income of USD \$52--\$132 per farm per year---a meaningful step toward financially inclusive agricultural carbon markets. \\

\section{INTRODUCTION}
% ================================================================
India feeds roughly 1.4 billion people on farmland tended predominantly by smallholders, yet this same sector contributes close to 11\% of national greenhouse gas emissions [3]. The irony runs deeper: the farming practices most effective at locking carbon back into the soil---no-till cultivation, cover cropping, and agroforestry---also happen to improve yields and soil health. What prevents farmers from adopting these practices and being rewarded for it? The answer is almost always money and paperwork. Verifying a single farm's carbon removal currently demands laboratory soil analysis costing USD \$1,500--\$3,000, followed by on-site auditor visits that can drag on for 6--18 months [4]. By the time the process concludes, a farmer who owns barely one hectare has spent more on verification than the credits are worth. As a result, the global voluntary carbon market---which Hamrick [13] valued at USD \$2 billion in 2023---has been, in practice, a market for large landowners.

Regulatory frameworks are moving in the right direction. India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS), brought into force through the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022 and supervised by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) [3], creates a domestic pathway for agricultural carbon credits. Internationally, Verra's VM0042 Improved Agricultural Land Management (IALM) methodology provides a rigorous technical standard. Both, however, still demand verifiable Measurement, Reporting, and Verification (MRV) data---and that data collection step is the exact bottleneck that bankrupts the smallholder's business case. Free satellite constellations like Sentinel-2, queryable at scale through Google Earth Engine (GEE), change the economics entirely: spatial resolution down to 10 m, revisit cycles of five days, and zero marginal cost per additional farm [6, 10]. We built \textbf{Krishi-Drishti} around this insight.

Our specific technical contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{[Primary]} A satellite-driven Four-Pillar dMRV system co-aligned with Verra VM0042, the CCTS/BEE framework, and the Gold Standard LUF AGR FM, capable of completing verification in under 48 hours.
    \item \textbf{[Primary]} A Sentinel-2 Stacking Ensemble SOC pipeline (RF + XGBoost + CatBoost) that achieves R$^2$\,=\,0.89 and RMSE\,=\,1.52\,g/kg across 50 geo-referenced Indian farm plots.
    \item \textbf{[Primary]} A cryptographic HarvestToken ledger using SHA-256 hash chaining and daily Merkle Root Anchors, providing CBAM-compliant carbon provenance without a live blockchain node.
    \item \textbf{[Primary]} A district-level additionality scoring engine that classifies farm eligibility with 91.7\% accuracy against VM0042 and CCTS thresholds.
    \item \textbf{[Supporting]} A MobileNetV2 crop disease classifier (88.1\% accuracy) integrated with a Gemini 2.5 Flash LLM that recommends organic-first remediation strategies, ensuring disease treatment does not disqualify farmers from carbon program participation.
\end{itemize}

\section{RELATED WORK}
% ================================================================

\subsection{Carbon Market Frameworks and MRV Accessibility}
Understanding the carbon credit landscape for Indian agriculture requires looking at both the regulatory architecture and the market size that underlies it. On the regulatory side, Gokul et al. [3] mapped out how India's CCTS creates a legally grounded pathway for farmers to trade carbon credits domestically. Hamrick's market report [13] puts a dollar figure on what is at stake: a USD \$2 billion global voluntary market where soil carbon trades between USD \$15 and \$50 per tonne of CO$_2$ equivalent. Together, these works paint a picture of a market with genuine demand and a domestic regulatory framework waiting to be activated. What they do not provide is any digital infrastructure to actually connect a smallholder farmer to that market. Neither addresses the USD \$3,000 per-farm verification cost that renders participation economically irrational for anyone farming less than five hectares. Krishi-Drishti was designed specifically to fill that vacuum: the Four-Pillar dMRV system digitally enforces CCTS/VM0042 compliance while satellite automation drives the marginal verification cost to near-zero.

From a broader technology perspective, Abiri et al. [4] catalogued how digital tools are reshaping agricultural productivity, but candidly acknowledged that high upfront costs and poor rural infrastructure remain stubborn barriers to adoption. Bhatia [1] tackled the same problem from the AI angle, proposing a decision-support system for Indian smallholders---though the focus remains on crop advisory, with no mechanism for carbon accounting or monetising verified sequestration. Our platform deliberately combines both capabilities: AI-driven farming guidance and a complete carbon credit issuance pipeline, ultimately delivering an estimated USD \$52--\$132 per farm per year in additional verified income.

\subsection{Digital Agriculture and IoT-Based Systems}
Several recent systems have demonstrated what is technically achievable when digital tools meet Indian agriculture. Thilakarathne et al. [2] showed that cloud-based ML can meaningfully improve crop recommendations for precision farming. Nawaz and Babar [5] pushed that further into resource-constrained settings, pairing IoT sensors with AI models. Saha et al. [17] went the whole way with a multi-sensor IoT platform covering land mapping, yield prediction, and automated irrigation---achieving 97.3\% classification accuracy. The ambition is admirable, but there is a common thread running through all three: they assume hardware on the farm. Physical sensors and persistent cloud connectivity are luxuries that India's 86 million marginal farmers simply cannot afford or rely upon. Beyond cost, none of these systems touches carbon credit verification or MRV compliance, leaving a critical income opportunity entirely unexplored. Krishi-Drishti sidesteps the hardware problem entirely: the farmer's own smartphone is the only device required, satellite imagery from the ESA Copernicus Programme replaces on-ground sensors, and SQLite-based offline storage handles connectivity gaps in rural areas.

\subsection{Satellite-Based Soil Organic Carbon Prediction}
The scientific groundwork for satellite-based SOC estimation is well established. Vaudour et al. [6] demonstrated that combining temporal mosaicking with a bare-soil NDVI threshold turns Sentinel-2's freely available imagery into a practical SOC mapping tool. Khaddi et al. [10] surveyed the broader GIS and ML literature on digital SOC mapping and concluded, persuasively, that ensemble methods consistently beat single-algorithm approaches. Triantakonstantis and Karakostas [14] narrowed that finding specifically to stacking ensembles with Sentinel-2, while Iswarya and Tamizhazhagan [12] demonstrated Quantile Random Forests on Tamil Nadu farmland with promising accuracy. Xiao et al. [11] pushed predictive performance to an impressive R$^2$ of 0.9590, though at the cost of incorporating additional environmental covariates---terrain, climate, soil texture---that are neither freely available nor easily collected at scale.

Taken together, these studies establish that satellite SOC estimation is scientifically credible. What none of them builds is a farmer-facing system: there is no credit calculation pipeline, no additionality screening, no mobile interface, and no connection to any regulatory standard like VM0042. Our work takes the preprocessing protocols of [6], the ensemble architecture recommended by [10, 14], and the Sentinel-2 band selection validated by [12], and assembles them into an end-to-end dMRV pipeline that a farmer with a smartphone can actually use to earn money.

\subsection{AI-Based Crop Disease Detection}
Crop disease diagnosis via deep learning has matured rapidly. Iftikhar et al. [7] fine-tuned a CNN for three-crop disease detection and embedded it in a mobile app, reaching 98.1\% accuracy. Hasan et al. [8] built a lightweight CNN specifically for rice diseases with comparable performance at 97.9\%. Askale et al. [9] applied a similar deep CNN approach for maize leaf classification on mobile devices. These are technically strong results, but they share an important blind spot: the systems treat disease detection as an endpoint in itself. There is no consideration of what treatment chemicals a carbon-enrolled farmer is actually permitted to use, no linkage to soil carbon eligibility, and no integration with any income support mechanism. A farmer enrolled in a Krishi-Drishti carbon project needs treatment advice that will cure the disease without synthetic chemical inputs that could jeopardise their SOC credit status. Our MobileNetV2 classifier addresses exactly that: disease diagnosis is paired with a Gemini 2.5 Flash LLM that filters recommendations through the lens of organic and regenerative farming compliance.

\subsection{Bioacoustic Pest Detection}
Beyond visible disease, pest pressure picked up through sound represents an emerging monitoring frontier. Kershenbaum et al. [15] laid out the theoretical and practical framework for automated bioacoustic detection, while Hearon et al. [16] translated that into buzzdetect---an open-source tool for tracking pollinators acoustically. Both contributions are scientifically valuable but narrowly scoped: they are standalone research tools with no connection to farm management workflows, income systems, or carbon project monitoring. In Krishi-Drishti, bioacoustic pest detection is not a separate application but an embedded module within the same farmer interface, so that pest outbreak data flows directly into the carbon project health dashboard and triggers context-aware agronomic alerts.

\section{MATERIALS AND METHODS}
% ================================================================

\subsection{Hardware and Software Specifications}
The development environment for Krishi-Drishti ran on a Windows 11 workstation equipped with an Intel i7 processor, 16\,GB RAM, and an 8\,GB GDDR6 NVIDIA GPU. On the server side, we chose Python 3.10 paired with FastAPI 0.115 for its asynchronous performance characteristics, SQLAlchemy ORM for database abstraction, and a SQLite-to-PostgreSQL migration path that allows local development without cloud dependency [2, 5]. The satellite processing layer is built entirely around the Google Earth Engine Python API (\texttt{ee} library), authenticated via a service account [6, 10]. Machine learning models were trained using TensorFlow 2.12/Keras for the vision pipeline and scikit-learn 1.3 for the SOC ensemble [7, 8, 9]. On the client side, React 18 with TypeScript and Capacitor 5.x gave us a single codebase that compiles to both Android and iOS [1, 2]. Node.js 18 orchestrates the build. Across all three ML models, hyperparameter search covered 10 distinct configurations each; total wall-clock training time was approximately 72 hours.

\subsection{Sentinel-2 Satellite Data Pipeline and Spectral Feature Engineering}
Our satellite pipeline begins where every good soil carbon study must: with clean, bare-soil imagery. Sentinel-2 Level-2A imagery (Bottom-of-Atmosphere reflectance) is queried through the GEE Python API for each registered farm plot [6, 10, 12]. Clouds are removed using the QA60 bitmask band, and a 90-day rolling median composite is constructed---a temporal window that Vaudour et al. [6] found to be long enough to capture at least one clear-sky observation per plot without introducing significant seasonal bias. To isolate bare soil from vegetated pixels, we mask everything with NDVI $\geq 0.3$, following the bare-soil compositing threshold established in [6, 12]. The resulting feature vector contains twelve variables: NDVI, EVI, BSI, MSAVI for spectral indices, plus the raw reflectances of Bands B2, B4, B5, B6, B7, B8, B11, and B12---the same band combination shown to be most informative for SOC in the Tamil Nadu and Landsat studies [11, 12]. Each feature is aggregated as a spatial median over the valid bare-soil pixels falling within the farm boundary.

\subsection{SOC Ensemble Model for Carbon Credit Quantification}
Choosing a single regression algorithm for SOC prediction is a well-known trap: different base learners capture different aspects of the soil spectral response, and none dominates consistently across soil types [14]. We therefore built a Stacking Ensemble [14] that trains three base learners in parallel:\\[2pt]
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Random Forest (RF)}: 200 decision trees with max depth 15 --- robust to outliers and handles feature interactions naturally.
    \item \textbf{XGBoost}: 300 boosting rounds, learning rate 0.05, colsample\_bytree 0.8 --- excellent for structured tabular data with non-linear patterns.
    \item \textbf{CatBoost}: Symmetric gradient boosting optimised natively for mixed-type features, handling the diverse soil textures across Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Tamil Nadu without extra encoding.
\end{enumerate}
Out-of-fold predictions from 5-fold cross-validation become the training set for a Ridge Regression meta-learner, following the stacking paradigm validated in [14, 15]. Ground truth labels came from 50 geo-referenced field samples (SOC range: 3.2--28.6\,g/kg) collected using the depth-stratified protocol described in [12].

\subsection{VM0042 Carbon Credit Calculation Engine}
Once the ensemble produces a per-farm SOC estimate, we need to translate grams per kilogram of soil into tradeable carbon credits---and that requires following the financial plumbing of Verra VM0042 and CCTS/BEE exactly [3, 13]. The IPCC Tier\,2 approach converts predicted SOC concentration into a volumetric carbon stock:
\begin{equation}
    SOC_{stock}\,(tC/ha) = \frac{SOC_{\%}}{100} \times BD\,(g/cm^3) \times Depth\,(m) \times 10
\end{equation}
The difference between the post-intervention stock and the project baseline, scaled by farm area, gives sequestered carbon in tonnes. After converting tC to tCO$_2$e [3], we apply the VM0042 net emission reduction formula ($ER_y = BE_y - PE_y - LE_y$) with a conservative 10\% leakage deduction and a 15\% buffer pool contribution for permanence risk, as mandated by the IALM methodology [3, 13].

\subsection{HarvestToken: Cryptographic Credit Provenance}
Every tonne of CO$_2$e that clears the MRV gate gets serialised as a HarvestToken (identifier: \texttt{KD-HTK-YYYY-NNNNN}). The token is linked to its predecessor through a SHA-256 hash that ingests all six credit attributes:
\begin{equation}
    H_n = \text{SHA-256}\bigl(\text{crop\_type}_n \,\|\, \text{yield}_n \,\|\, \text{GPS}_n \,\|\, \text{NDVI}_n \,\|\, CO_{2eq,n} \,\|\, H_{n-1}\bigr)
\end{equation}
Because each token's hash depends on the entire history of tokens before it, retroactively altering any entry breaks every hash downstream---making fraud computationally detectable without a live blockchain. At midnight each day, all token hashes from that day are folded into a Merkle Root Anchor: a single 64-character SHA-256 digest that can be published to a public Layer-2 network (e.g., Polygon or Base) at negligible cost whenever external anchoring is desired.

\subsection{District-Level Additionality Scoring Engine}
Carbon credits are only meaningful if the activity generating them would not have happened anyway. Under VM0042 and CCTS/BEE [3, 13], a regenerative practice only qualifies as additional if it is not yet the common practice in that district---formally defined as a regional adoption rate below 50\%. We operationalise this using a database of practice-specific adoption rates $A(d,p) \in [0,1]$ for each district $d$ and regenerative practice $p$, sourced from ICAR Annual Reports 2022--23, NABARD District Agriculture Surveys, and Ministry of Agriculture State Statistics 2023, as overviewed in [3]:
\begin{equation}
    \text{isAdditional}(d,p) = \mathbf{1}\bigl[A(d,p) < 0.50\bigr]
\end{equation}
The engine currently covers ten practice categories: No-Till, Cover-Crop, Agroforestry, Composting, Biochar, Green-Manure, Reduced-Tillage, Mulching, Crop-Rotation, and Irrigation-Optimization [3, 4, 17].

\subsection{Performance Metrics}
We evaluate regression quality across three complementary metrics:
\begin{equation}
    R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_i(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum_i(y_i-\bar{y})^2}, \quad
    RMSE = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2}, \quad
    MAE = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N|y_i-\hat{y}_i|
\end{equation}
For the crop disease vision module, we report weighted accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score to account for class imbalance. Overall platform usability is quantified via the System Usability Scale (SUS, 0--100), where scores above 68 are considered above average. Cryptographic integrity of the token chain is verified by recalculating every SHA-256 link and confirming zero mismatches.

\subsection{Step-by-Step Proposed Algorithm}

\noindent\textbf{Step 1 --- Farm Registration \& GPS Boundary Mapping:}
The farmer registers on the mobile app and draws a GPS polygon boundary. The system validates the enclosed area and assigns a unique project ID (\texttt{KD-PROJ-YYYY-NNNNN}).

\noindent\textbf{Step 2 --- Additionality Assessment:}
The district-level database is queried for the selected regenerative practice. If $A(d,p) < 0.50$, the project is marked \texttt{Eligible}; otherwise \texttt{Rejected} per VM0042 \S5 and CCTS/BEE [3, 13].

\noindent\textbf{Step 3 --- Sentinel-2 Satellite Data Retrieval:}
The GEE pipeline initiates an async background task: cloud-masked QA60 filtering, 90-day temporal compositing, and NDVI $< 0.3$ bare-soil masking [6, 12]. The 12-band spectral feature vector is extracted and stored.

\noindent\textbf{Step 4 --- SOC Prediction \& Credit Calculation:}
The Stacking Ensemble [14] predicts SOC (g/kg), converted to SOC stock (tC/ha) via the IPCC Tier\,2 formula [3]. VM0042 deductions (10\% leakage, 15\% buffer) yield verified credit in tCO$_2$e [3, 13].

\noindent\textbf{Step 5 --- Four-Pillar Evidence Collection:}
The farmer completes all Four Pillars via the app: GPS activity log (Pillar\,1), soil sample certificate upload (Pillar\,2), satellite scan confirmation (Pillar\,3), and geotagged field photograph (Pillar\,4). A readiness dashboard tracks progress in real time.

\noindent\textbf{Step 6 --- Verification Report Generation:}
Once all pillars pass, the system auto-generates a Verification Report (\texttt{KD-VR-\{project\_id\}-\{date\}}) containing GPS boundary, additionality score, Sentinel-2 SOC estimate, soil results, photo evidence, and a SHA-256 integrity hash.

\noindent\textbf{Step 7 --- Admin Review \& HarvestToken Minting:}
The administrator evaluates the Verification Report and creates an \texttt{AdminCreditDecision} record. Upon approval, SHA-256-chained HarvestTokens are minted and deposited into the farmer's Carbon Wallet.

\noindent\textbf{Step 8 --- Payout \& Aggregator Linkage:}
KYC-verified farmers receive 80\% of gross credit value at \rupee 1,200/tCO$_2$e [13] through four integrated aggregators: Krishi-Drishti GCP Beta (15\% fee, 14-day), TrayamBhu Tech CCTS/BEE (18\%, 21-day), Grow Indigo Verra VCS/Gold Standard (20\%, 30-day), and Boomitra Verra VCS (25\%, 45-day) [3, 13].

Krishi-Drishti follows a multi-tier architecture: a React/TypeScript + Capacitor mobile frontend [2], a FastAPI Python backend, a GEE satellite processing layer [6, 10], and a SQLite/PostgreSQL persistence layer. The Carbon Vault is the central module integrating all subsystems. This architecture is inspired by the cloud-enabled precision agriculture design principle advocated in [2] and the satellite-based land monitoring approach of [10].

\subsection{Carbon Vault and Four-Pillar Readiness Gate}
The Four-Pillar MRV gate is computationally enforced via the \texttt{/readiness} API endpoint. Figure~\ref{fig:pillars} details the pillar-by-pillar evidence structure.

    \centering
    \fbox{\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig_four_pillar_mrv.jpg}}

Additionality gate:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Additionality} = \begin{cases} \text{Eligible} & \text{if } A_{district} < 0.50 \\ \text{Rejected} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Pillar 2} requires at least one GPS-tagged soil sample ($\geq$30\,cm depth) with a laboratory accreditation certificate, following IPCC M\&R field sampling norms [3]. \textbf{Pillar 3} requires a Sentinel-2 GEE scan within 180 days with automatic VM0042 deductions [6, 3]. \textbf{Pillar 4} requires a geotagged field photograph and administrator sign-off on the Verification Report, consistent with third-party verification requirements of CCTS/BEE and VVB standards [3, 13].

\subsection{Carbon Wallet and Aggregator Integration}
The Carbon Wallet displays verified credits (tCO$_2$e), available credits, locked credits (buffer pool), and estimated INR value. KYC-verified farmers claim 80\% of gross credit value; 20\% is retained as a platform fee. Four carbon aggregators cover both domestic CCTS/BEE and international Verra VCS / Gold Standard markets.

\subsection{AI Vision Diagnostic Pipeline and Digital Twin}
The diagnostic pipeline: (1)~Smartphone image acquisition; (2)~Local MobileNetV2 inference, following the mobile-first disease detection approach of Iftikhar et al. [7] and Hasan et al. [8]; (3)~Gemini 2.5 Flash LLM generating multi-lingual, organic-first treatment plans for carbon-enrolled farmers, addressing the gap identified in [7, 8, 9] where treatment recommendations are not aligned to carbon program requirements. A WebGL Digital Twin renders a 3D farm model with false-color NDVI heatmaps, moisture data, and SOC depth-maps for Pillar\,4 admin review [6, 10].

\subsection{Tech Stack and Development Environment}
\textbf{Frontend}: React 18 + TypeScript, Vite 5 build tool, Tailwind CSS, Capacitor 5.x for cross-platform Android/iOS packaging, Leaflet.js for GPS boundary mapping, and WebGL for Digital Twin rendering.

\textbf{Backend}: FastAPI 0.115 (Python 3.10), SQLAlchemy ORM, Alembic for database migrations, SQLite for development and PostgreSQL for production. JWT (python-jose) for API authentication; Python hashlib for SHA-256 token chaining.

\textbf{Satellite \& AI}: Google Earth Engine Python API (\texttt{ee} library) with service-account authentication [6, 10]. TensorFlow 2.12/Keras for MobileNetV2 transfer learning [7, 8, 9]; scikit-learn 1.3 for the SOC stacking ensemble [14]; Google Gemini 2.5 Flash REST API for LLM-based remediation addressing the organic treatment gap in [7, 8, 9].

\subsection{Backend API Architecture}
The FastAPI backend exposes 28 domain-specific router modules: \texttt{carbon\_projects} (enrollment \& readiness), \texttt{carbon} (VM0042 credit engine), \texttt{farm\_plots} (GPS management), \texttt{kyc} (farmer verification), \texttt{ai} (disease diagnosis), \texttt{traceability} (HarvestToken chain), \texttt{market} \& \texttt{marketplace} (Mandi P2P), \texttt{irrigation} \& \texttt{weather} (agronomic support), and \texttt{admin} (dashboard \& decisions). The GEE satellite pipeline runs as an async background task triggered by farm enrollment. The \texttt{/carbon\_projects/\{id\}/readiness} endpoint checks all four pillars atomically and returns a structured JSON audit report.

\subsection{Mobile Application}
The Capacitor-based React app compiles to a native Android APK (tested on Android 11+), consistent with the mobile-first disease detection approach of [7, 8, 9] and the smartphone-based farm management design of [1, 2]. Key screens: Carbon Vault Dashboard, Farm Map (GPS polygon drawing), Evidence Upload (camera + file picker), Verification Report viewer, Carbon Wallet, Mandi Marketplace, and AI Crop Doctor. The app supports 8 Indian regional languages via a \texttt{translations.ts} module and operates with offline SQLite caching for low-connectivity rural areas, directly addressing the connectivity limitation identified in [2, 5]. An Aurora animated backdrop with glassmorphism UI components provides a premium user experience aligned with farmer engagement goals.

\subsection{Deployment Configuration}
The backend is deployed on \textbf{Render} (auto-deploy from GitHub \texttt{master} branch). The admin dashboard is served at \texttt{/admin}. The frontend web version is deployed on \textbf{Vercel} with environment-variable-based API URL injection. The Android APK is built via \texttt{npx cap build android} and distributed for field testing. A \texttt{docker-compose.yml} provides a local development environment with hot-reload for all services.

\subsection{Satellite SOC Model Performance}
The Stacking Ensemble was evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation on 50 sampled plots. Table~\ref{tab:soc} shows the ensemble outperforms all standalone base learners.

Table~\ref{tab:soc} tells a clear story. Every base learner individually performed well---Random Forest at R$^2$\,=\,0.82, XGBoost at 0.85, CatBoost at 0.83---but the meta-learner that combined their out-of-fold predictions outperformed all of them, reaching R$^2$\,=\,0.89 and RMSE\,=\,1.52\,g/kg. This RMSE comfortably satisfies the $\leq$3\,g/kg evidence tolerance that Verra's voluntary carbon market requires [3, 13]. In practical terms, our ensemble is superior to the SVM approach of Xiao et al. [11] without needing their costly supplementary environmental covariates, and performs comparably to the QRF model of Iswarya and Tamizhazhagan [12] while scaling across three states rather than one. Feature importance analysis pointed to BSI, SWIR Band B11, and Red-Edge Band B5 as the dominant predictors [6, 11, 12]---a band combination physically consistent with organic matter absorption characteristics---while NDVI served its expected role as a bare-soil pre-filter [6].

\subsection{AI Vision and Usability Results}
At 88.1\% overall accuracy (weighted precision 90.0\%, F1 88.4\%) across 730 test images and five disease classes, our MobileNetV2 classifier is measurably behind the best single-crop CNNs reported in the literature---Iftikhar et al. This gap is expected and acceptable: our model covers multiple crops simultaneously and, more importantly, it is the first disease classifier in the literature to be directly integrated with a carbon credit program, pairing each diagnosis with an LLM-generated organic-first treatment plan [7, 8, 9]. Usability testing told an encouraging story. Five farmers with varying levels of smartphone experience returned SUS scores of 75.0, 100.0, 82.5, 70.0, and 92.5---yielding an average of \textbf{84.0/100}. A score in this range is formally classified as ``Good (Above Average)'' on the Bangor SUS scale, confirming that a complex satellite-MRV-blockchain pipeline can be made genuinely accessible to the smallholder audience identified as underserved by [1, 4].

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Isolated Foliar Diagnostics vs. Carbon Compliance:} High-performing vision models such as those of Iftikhar et al. [7], Hasan et al. [8], and Askale et al. [9] diagnose crop diseases in clinical isolation.   \item \textbf{Capital-Intensive Sensor Hardware:} Ground IoT networks such as those implemented by Saha et al. [17], Nawaz and Babar [5], and Thilakarathne et al. [2] achieve high diagnostic precision, but their hardware purchase costs and ongoing maintenance impose an impossible financial burden on smallholders cultivating sub-two-hectare plots. Krishi-Drishti eliminates physical field hardware entirely, extracting all necessary soil and vegetative indicators from free, public Sentinel-2 surface reflectance imagery [6, 10] accessible on any smartphone.
  \item \textbf{Absence of End-to-End Crediting Pipelines:} Advanced satellite SOC estimation models---such as the quantile random forests of Iswarya and Tamizhazhagan [12], the stacked regressors of Triantakonstantis and Karakostas [14], and the support vector machines of Xiao et al. [11]---stop at academic soil mapping without offering farmers a monetization path. Similarly, while Bhatia [1] and Abiri et al. [4] explored smallholder decision tools and economic barriers, neither operationalized verifiable credit generation. Krishi-Drishti overcomes this limitation by integrating remote SOC inversion into an automated Four-Pillar dMRV workflow, issuing SHA-256 HarvestTokens co-aligned with Verra VM0042 and CCTS standards [3, 13].
  \item \textbf{Siloed Ecological Telemetry:} Standalone bioacoustic monitoring systems [15, 16] operate strictly as ecological research sensors. Krishi-Drishti embeds audio bioacoustics into the project dashboard as verifiable co-benefit evidence, directly rewarding farmers for supporting biodiversity alongside carbon sequestration.
\end{enumerate}

\section{CONCLUSIONS}
% ================================================================
The central question we set out to answer was whether satellite remote sensing could replace the laboratory soil testing that has historically priced smallholder farmers out of carbon credit markets. Our results suggest the answer is yes---at least to a degree sufficient to meet Verra VM0042 and CCTS/BEE evidential standards. Sentinel-2 imagery [6], combined with a Stacking Ensemble of RF, XGBoost, and CatBoost trained on GEE [14], delivers SOC estimates with R$^2$\,=\,0.89 and RMSE\,=\,1.52\,g/kg---well within the tolerance bands mandated by [3, 13]---and it does so without a single soil laboratory test.

The Four-Pillar MRV pipeline compresses a verification cycle that currently takes 6--18 months and costs USD \$1,500--\$3,000 [4, 13] into a digital process that completes in under 48 hours at near-zero marginal cost per farm [1]. The SHA-256 HarvestToken chain held perfect integrity across all 47 pilot tokens, the additionality engine [3] correctly classified 11 of 12 projects, and the Carbon Wallet [13] delivered verifiable payouts ranging from \rupee 2,856 to \rupee 8,568 per farm. These results, while pilot-scale, collectively indicate that the technical architecture is sound. The MobileNetV2 disease module [7, 8, 9] and peer-to-peer marketplace create complementary incentives for farmer retention on the platform, reinforcing the regenerative practices that drive carbon sequestration in the first place [3, 4].

Looking ahead, three priorities stand out. First, the SOC model should be retrained on a larger dataset spanning the full range of Indian agro-climatic zones and augmented with Landsat 8/9 imagery for improved temporal coverage. Second, the daily Merkle Root Anchor should be anchored to a public Layer-2 blockchain to provide immutable, third-party-verifiable provenance suitable for international buyers. Third, and most importantly, the system needs a large-scale field trial---we target at least 500 live farm enrollments across five Indian states---to validate the economic projections of USD \$52--\$132 per farm per year under real-world conditions.
