1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Project Overview

Krishi-Drishti is a verified carbon credit platform designed to enable Indian farmers to generate, track, and monetize carbon credits through sustainable agricultural practices. The platform integrates satellite data, farmer engagement tools, and blockchain-based verification to create a trusted ecosystem for carbon credit generation.

1.2 Problem Statement

Indian agriculture contributes 18% of national GDP and employs 58% of the workforce, but faces two critical challenges:

• Carbon Emissions Crisis: Agricultural practices emit 16-20% of India's total greenhouse gases through soil degradation, methane emissions, and land use changes.

• Farmer Income Gap: Small and marginal farmers (86% of agricultural population) lack access to climate finance and carbon offset income despite having potential to sequester significant carbon.

Current barriers preventing farmer participation in carbon markets:
- No standardized verification mechanism for farming practices
- Lack of trust in satellite-based analysis
- Complex aggregator systems requiring extensive documentation
- No transparent payment infrastructure
- Regulatory uncertainty in India's carbon market

1.3 Solution Approach

Krishi-Drishti addresses these barriers through:

• Real Satellite Verification: Google Earth Engine (Sentinel-2 + SMAP) provides real-time vegetation and soil moisture monitoring with 86% confidence scores.

• Multi-Layer Verification: Combines satellite data, farmer practice logs, photo evidence, baseline soil testing, and risk scoring.

• Transparent Aggregator Network: Direct integration with Boomitra, EKI Energy, and emerging carbon aggregators.

• Farmer-First Design: Mobile-first interface, Hindi language support, UPI payment integration.

• Registry Compliance: Pathway to Verra Registry and Gold Standard certification for global carbon market access.

1.4 Target Users

Primary: Small/marginal farmers (0.5-5 hectares) in India practicing agroforestry, no-till, cover cropping, or forestry.

Secondary: Agricultural cooperatives, NGOs, aggregators seeking verified carbon credits.

Tertiary: Corporate buyers seeking ESG compliance and carbon offset portfolios.

1.5 Expected Outcomes

The platform targets:
• Scalable verification of 1000+ farmers
• Direct farmer participation in global carbon markets
• Registry-certified carbon credit generation
• Sustainable revenue model for ongoing operations

2. LITERATURE SURVEY

2.1 Existing Systems

2.1.1 Current Global Carbon Market Landscape

Verra Registry (formerly VCS):
- 560+ projects worldwide, 300+ million credits issued
- Requires third-party auditor validation
- 18-24 month verification cycle
- $15,000-50,000 project registration costs
- Advantages: Global acceptance, price transparency ($12-20/ton)
- Disadvantages: High barrier to entry for smallholder farmers

Gold Standard:
- 2,000+ projects, 120+ million credits
- 12% price premium over Verra (SDG alignment)
- Faster 6-9 month verification
- Advantages: ESG market acceptance, climate finance linkage
- Disadvantages: Still costly, farmer engagement minimal

CDM (Clean Development Mechanism):
- 7,800+ projects in developing countries
- India's largest carbon credit program
- 2+ billion credits issued
- Disadvantages: Declining market interest, complex methodology

2.1.2 Existing Agricultural Carbon Platforms

Grow Indigo (USA):
- Technology: Satellite monitoring + annual field audits
- Market: 500k acres, USA-focused
- Farmers: Paid $1-5/acre/year for data
- Verification: Hybrid (satellite + ground truth)
- Limitation: USA-only, farmer earning limited

Boomitra (India - DIRECT COMPETITOR):
- Technology: Sentinel-2 + farmer mobile app + IoT sensors
- Market: 100k+ farmers, India focus
- Verification: Satellite + practice logs + spot audits (10-15%)
- Payment: $10-30 per farmer per ton of carbon
- Strength: India-native, local language support, aggregator partnerships

EKI Energy (India - DIRECT COMPETITOR):
- Technology: Satellite + full-scale audits + carbon modeling
- Market: 250k acres across South Asia
- Verification: 3-layer (satellite, audit, chemistry)
- Payment: Tiered by farmer verification level
- Strength: Enterprise buyer network, institutional credibility

TrayamBhu (India):
- Technology: Community-based + satellite + IoT
- Market: 50k farmers, regional focus
- Verification: Local auditors + satellite data
- Limitation: Limited scalability, community-dependent

Agricultural Carbon Sequestration Potential (India):

4.2 Verification Process Design

See diagrams/verification_process.md for detailed verification pipeline.

Five-Layer Verification:

Layer 1 - Satellite Data (Day 1):
Input: Farm coordinates, area
Process: Query Earth Engine for Sentinel-2 + SMAP data
Output: NDVI score, soil moisture, confidence (0-1)

Layer 2 - Practice Verification (Day 2-7):
Input: Farmer logs practice (date, type, photos)
Process: Validate photos (geolocation, timestamp)
Output: Practice confirmed or flagged

Layer 3 - Baseline Comparison (Day 1-7):
Input: Pre-project soil carbon (lab test)
Process: Calculate delta vs. satellite baseline
Output: Additionality score

Layer 4 - Risk Assessment (Day 7):
Input: All verification data
Process: ML model (satellite vs. claimed, geolocation stability, outlier detection)
Output: Risk score (0-100), approval recommendation

Layer 5 - Aggregator & Registry (Day 7-30):
Input: Verified farmer package
Process: Submit to aggregator, then to registry
Output: Credit serialization, market listing

4.3 Carbon Credit Calculation

Methodology: CCTS (Custom Carbon Tracking System) compliant

Base Calculation:
carbon_credits = (baseline_soc - satellite_soc) * area_hectares * density_factor

Where:
- baseline_soc: Soil organic carbon (kg/ha) from pre-project baseline
- satellite_soc: Current SOC derived from satellite NDVI
- area_hectares: Farm plot size
- density_factor: Practice-specific multiplier

Practice Multipliers:
- No-till: 1.2x (proven carbon sequestration boost)
- Cover-cropping: 1.1x
- Agroforestry: 1.5x
- Composting: 0.8x

Buffer Pool Deduction:
buffer_credits = gross_credits * 15%
issuable_credits = gross_credits * 85%

Risk Adjustment:
final_credits = issuable_credits * (1 - risk_discount)

Where risk_discount ranges 0-40% based on risk_score.
